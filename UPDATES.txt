TRIPAURA BACKLOG (UPDATES.txt)
==============================

The owner's list of what to build next, kept in the repository root and committed to git.
Owner: Ranjan Verma. Every developer and every AI agent working on TripAura reads this file at the start of
every prompt or task, before doing anything else (the full routine is in HANDOFF.md section 0).


HOW TO USE THIS FILE
--------------------
1. Read the whole file at the start of every task, even when the task is about something else.
2. Build the PLANNED items in order, one or a few at a time, each as its own release with a version number
   (HANDOFF.md section 13: minor for a feature, major for a new way of using the product). Never build them all
   at once.
3. When an item is finished: delete it from PLANNED, add one line with its version under DONE, and renumber the
   rest. When it is partly finished: rewrite it so it says only what is left.
4. When the owner adds a rough note anywhere in this file, rewrite it into the same shape as the items below
   (goal, what to build, done when, size), keeping all of its meaning and anything specific he asked for.
5. Ideas that an agent notices while working (a bug it could not fix, a clear improvement) go under SUGGESTIONS.
   Suggestions are not approved work: build one only when the owner asks for it, then move it to PLANNED.
6. Commit changes to this file in the same commit as the work they describe, and mention them in the HANDOFF.md
   change log.
7. Anything here that touches secrets, money, cloud resources or Chinmay's deployment files still needs the
   owner's yes in chat first (see CLAUDE.md section 6).


PLANNED (in this order)
-----------------------

1. Guided chat: another way into a plan
   Goal: a person who does not want a form can plan a trip by answering a short conversation.
   Already there: the five-step planner, the "Or just say it" box (one sentence fills the planner), the hero
   quick start (city and days), picking the stay on a map (v2.5.0) and must-see places (v2.7.0).
   What to build: a chat-style flow that asks one question at a time (city, dates, who is going, budget, what they
   like, must-sees), shows each answer on the boarding pass as it goes, understands free text with the existing
   trip parser (backend/app/nlp/trip_parser.py), and ends in the same trip request the planner sends. No paid
   AI is required; an optional AI layer must stay optional, like the AI summary.
   Done when: a whole trip can be planned from the chat alone on desktop and phone, and the result is the same
   kind of saved trip as from the planner.
   Size: minor release.

2. Plan each day on a real graph
   Goal: better day plans (less travel, fewer awkward detours) by treating places and travel times as a graph.
   Today: backend/app/engines/itinerary.py picks a day anchor, adds stops greedily using straight-line travel
   estimates, then improves the order with 2-opt; real travel legs are only fetched afterwards.
   What to build: first check how far the current planner already behaves like a graph search, then rebuild
   the ordering on a proper graph (places as nodes, estimated travel minutes as edge weights, opening hours and
   meal windows as constraints), for example a time-windowed route search instead of greedy plus 2-opt.
   Done when: on the same inputs the new planner travels less or fits more good places without breaking hours,
   meals or must-sees, shown by tests that compare old and new plans, and planning time stays about the same
   (a 7-day Lisbon plan takes about 1 s today).
   Size: minor release (the request and response stay the same).

3. Multi-destination trips (major release, v3.0.0)
   Goal: plan one trip across several cities, for example A to B to C.
   What to build: choose the cities, their order and the days in each; plan the travel between cities (mode,
   time, cost, labelled as estimates like everything else); each city keeps today's day-by-day planning; the
   budget covers the whole trip.
   Before building: work out the flow that is most comfortable for the traveller (how they add and reorder
   cities, how days are split, how the trip page shows several cities) and agree it with the owner.
   Done when: a saved trip can hold several cities with travel days between them, on desktop and phone, and
   old single-city trips still open.
   Size: major release, v3.0.0.

4. Research similar products and propose what makes TripAura stand out
   Goal: find what itinerary planners and travel sites lack, and turn that into ideas that make TripAura
   clearly different, both in features and in the interface.
   What to do: study headout.com and other trip and itinerary planners (for example Wanderlog, TripIt, Google
   Travel, Layla, Roam Around, Tripadvisor trips, Visit A City, Inspirock). Look at every main page and flow:
   home, planner, trip page, maps, sharing, phone layout. Note what each does well and what all of them lack.
   Output: a written comparison and a short list of innovative ideas (features and bold interface ideas that
   none of them have), each with why it matters and a rough size, shared with the owner in chat. Nothing is
   built until the owner picks from the list; the picked ideas then become PLANNED items here.
   Done when: the owner has the comparison and the ranked ideas.
   Size: research only, no release.


SUGGESTIONS (from agents, not approved; build only when the owner asks)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

- Must-see cafes and restaurants. A must-see is matched only against sights, so "Hawa Mahal Cafe" pinned near
  Hawa Mahal is taken as the palace, and an unmatched cafe is planned as a sight with assumed hours and an
  estimated entry fee. Match food places too and plan them as a meal or a short stop. (Found in the v2.7.2
  review.)
- Automated browser tests for the frontend. There is no frontend test runner; every release is checked by hand
  in a headless browser. A small Playwright suite (plan a trip, open it, select a place, edit, regenerate,
  delete and undo, at desktop and phone width) would catch regressions in CI.
- Show progress while a new city loads. A first visit to a city takes 10 to 20 s with only a timer; loading the
  city in the background and polling its real stages would show what is happening.
- Place photos from Wikimedia Commons (Wikidata P18), with a blurred placeholder while they load.
- Remaining small performance items (HANDOFF.md section 10): each place query in open mode re-filters the whole
  city list; in live or OSRM mode transport options for a leg are fetched one after another; dish matching
  scans reviews twice.
- For Chinmay (deployment, needs his and the owner's yes): confirm TRUSTED_PROXY_COUNT in production, since
  with 1 behind Vercel plus Azure every visitor may share one rate-limit bucket (HANDOFF.md section 10).
