1. Guided chat. Another way into a plan: a short conversation that asks one thing at a time, fills the boarding pass as it goes, uses the existing trip parser, and ends in the same trip as the planner.

2. Plan with a real graph. The planner now picks a day anchor, adds stops greedily and tidies the order with 2-opt. Rebuild that part on a proper graph (places and travel times, with opening hours and meals as limits), and show with tests that it travels less or fits more without being slower.

3. Multi-destination trips (major update, v3.0.0). Plan A to B to C in one trip: choose the order and days per stop, and plan the travel between cities. Work out the flow that is most comfortable for the traveller and agree it with the owner before building it.

4. Research similar sites. Study headout.com and other itinerary planners (Wanderlog, TripIt, Google Travel and more) page by page, find what they all lack, and give the owner a short list of innovative features and bold UI ideas that would make TripAura stand out. Research only; nothing is built until the owner picks.

5. More deep learning where it really helps. The project already runs a small local embedding model (bge-small through fastembed, backend/app/nlp/semantic.py) for interest matching and review sentiment. Find other places where a learned model would clearly improve the plans or the experience (for example ranking places from their descriptions, guessing how long a visit takes, reading reviews for dishes, or understanding the free-text trip box better), build only what measurably helps, keep it free, local and optional, and keep the planner working without it.
